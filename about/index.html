About John Mwaura

I am John Mwaura, known online as Dev John, an independent web and software developer in Kenya. I have worked since 2023, building business websites, online stores, custom software and automation. I also founded ABC Systems.

I work across the full product: what a visitor sees, the database behind it, the APIs that connect it to other tools and the deployment. That can mean a small website with a clear enquiry route, or a business application with accounts, reports and payment workflows.

My work starts with the task someone needs to complete. A shop customer wants to find an item and place an order. A member of staff needs the right information to do their job. A citizen looking through public spending needs records that are understandable and searchable. Those needs shape the interface and the code.

What I build

For a business website, I focus on clear service information, useful pages and an easy next action. A visitor on a phone should be able to understand what you offer and contact you without searching through a crowded layout. See my business website development service.

An online store has a different job. Products need categories and useful details, and the cart, checkout and order handling need to fit the business. I work with M-Pesa and Stripe where they suit the agreed scope. My e-commerce development page explains the starter store.

Custom software brings forms, business rules, reporting and an admin interface together around a specific workflow. I build dashboards and internal tools as well as public applications. Read about custom software development and the difference between a small scoped project and a larger platform.

Automation connects actions that would otherwise need repeated manual work. That can include capturing an enquiry from WhatsApp, sending an order notification or syncing information with an existing tool. The WhatsApp automation service covers those possibilities.

UWAZI: public finance people can explore

UWAZI is my flagship public finance transparency platform for Kenya. It makes government expenditure searchable and brings together React, TypeScript, Node.js, PostgreSQL and Supabase. It shows my interest in civic technology and in making complex records usable through a public interface.

For a project like this, the data structure matters as much as the page design. The search experience depends on how information is organised behind the screen. You can open the UWAZI project entry and its live platform to explore the work.
